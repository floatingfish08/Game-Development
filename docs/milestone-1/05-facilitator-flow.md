# Facilitator Flow

## Facilitator's job

The application handles distribution, clocks, state, and outcome rules. The
facilitator provides presence: establish the fiction, keep decisions moving,
observe behavior, apply pressure, protect participant safety, and lead the
debrief. They do not perform hidden arithmetic or impersonate every system.

## Before the session

1. Create a session and select six- or seven-player mode.
2. Copy the participant link and Teams/Zoom link.
3. Confirm microphone/headphone expectations and modern-browser support.
4. Open the facilitator console and shared station display.
5. Confirm all roles show Connected.
6. Read the safety note and explain the private-message off-ramp.
7. Start the role check; each player reads only their responsibility line aloud.

Suggested safety language:

> This is a fictional survival-horror leadership exercise involving emergency
> pressure, a missing colleague, contamination, entrapment, and implied historic
> deaths. Message me privately if you need to pause or step out. You will not be
> asked to explain why.

## Player briefing (5 minutes)

Tell players:

- They are tonight's crew at Blackout Ridge.
- Their private terminal contains information others may not receive.
- They may share or paraphrase anything they see.
- Every stage ends with one crew commitment submitted by the Station Lead.
- Running out of time causes a consequence; it does not remove anyone from play.
- They do not need external technical knowledge.

Do not explain `LOWER ROUTE`, the Lark Shift, the lower hold, the status metaphor,
or the AI learning objective.

## Live cue sheet

| Stage | Facilitator opening cue | Observe | Intervention threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | “The outgoing crew left the handover unfinished. The station is filling the gaps.” | Who challenges fluent defaults? Who asks about sources? | At 90 seconds with no discussion, trigger `SOURCE AGE AVAILABLE` |
| 2 | “Backup power is stable. Capacity is not.” | What does the crew protect instinctively? | If dependencies confuse players, trigger Systems summary—not a recommendation |
| 3 | “There is traffic beneath the bulletin. It may be noise; preserving it has a cost.” | Clean authority versus weak signal; confidence language | If treated as a cipher, remind them the decision is what to retain, not decode everything |
| 4 | “You have one respirator and a five-minute external window.” | Is the test bounded? Is there an abort rule? | If planning stalls, prompt: “What single assumption are you testing?” |
| 5 | “The conduit ends at a hatch beneath your floor.” | How the team updates its frame; lock safeguards | Before an irreversible descent, ensure the UI previews containment risk |
| 6 | “Cordon Control hears Ridge, but does not yet believe Ridge.” | Evidence specificity and receiver needs | If prose-writing stalls, reveal the five message slots |
| 7 | “The hold is sealing. The station is filing its final status.” | Prioritisation across people, lock, and signal | No strategy hints; only clarify available actions and costs |

## Controls required in the facilitator console

- Start, pause, resume, and advance stage
- Add 30 seconds for technical recovery
- Show one contextual hint at a time
- Reassign a disconnected role
- Re-send the current private card
- Mark a participant temporarily absent
- Safety pause with neutral shared screen
- View current tracks, resources, evidence flags, and pending consequence
- Override an incorrectly recorded choice with an audit note
- Preview and confirm the calculated ending
- Generate a debrief summary from observed decisions

The facilitator cannot secretly change an ending merely to reward the group. An
override is for technical/content ambiguity and remains visible in the session log.

## Pacing rules

- Start each stage with no more than 30 seconds of spoken setup.
- Let the interface deliver system language and audio.
- Give the first time warning halfway and the final warning at 30 seconds.
- Ask the Station Lead for a commitment when conversation becomes circular.
- Use at most one designed hint per stage before allowing the normal consequence.
- Never explain an earlier “mistake” during play. Show its effect and keep moving.

## Debrief (20 minutes)

The facilitator dashboard produces a timeline containing held defaults, powered
systems, preserved/discarded signals, field test, hatch plan, correction, finale
assignments, and the difference between final system and human statuses.

### 1. Immediate reaction — 2 minutes

- What felt most dangerous?
- When did your understanding of Lower Route change?

### 2. Reconstruct the failure — 4 minutes

Place the final system status beside the human outcome.

- What did `OPERATIONAL` actually measure?
- Which clean label did we trust for longest?
- What evidence was present before we were ready to believe it?

### 3. Role map — 4 minutes

Ask each player what their role protected. Connect the six lenses to self-readiness,
leadership fluency, roadmap/learning, value discovery, people/adoption, and
risk/guardrails. The seventh role demonstrates cross-functional communication.

### 4. Hesitation diagnosis — 4 minutes

- Where was caution productive?
- Where did waiting allow a default to harden?
- Did uncertainty come from evidence, risk, people, ownership, control, or feeling
  technically exposed?

Frame profiles as behaviors observed in one exercise, not personality diagnoses.

### 5. Workplace bridge — 4 minutes

- Where does a dashboard, Copilot, vendor tool, automated workflow, or informal AI
  practice produce a clean output whose underlying evidence is unclear?
- Who could disappear from that output?

### 6. First step — 2 minutes

Each participant completes:

> One label or output I will challenge is ____. The first bounded check I will
> make is ____. The person or evidence I will keep visible is ____.

End on the participant's action, not a product pitch. The facilitator can then
transition naturally to the AI-Ready Leader course offer.

## Safety and accessibility behavior

- Safety pause stops clocks and replaces private/shared content with a neutral
  holding screen.
- A participant may observe rather than take direct action after opting out.
- All audio content has captions/transcripts.
- No jump scares, flashing light effects, or mandatory camera use.
- The facilitator guide flags contamination, missing-person, entrapment, and
  implied-death content before play.
